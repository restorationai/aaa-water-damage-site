Call your building's emergency maintenance or AOAO line next since the leak can travel into the unit below, then call a plumber for the tank itself. You open the water heater closet in your Kakaako or Waikiki high-rise unit and find an inch of water creeping toward the hallway, or your downstairs neighbor is already knocking because water is coming through their ceiling. Either way, the clock matters more here than almost anywhere else on Oahu. A failed tank on the twelfth floor doesn't just ruin your flooring, it can soak a neighbor's ceiling and electrical within minutes. Stop the water at the source first. Then work through the list below in order.

## What do you do in the first five minutes of a condo water heater leak?

Shut off the cold-water supply valve at the top of the tank, cut power at the breaker feeding the water heater, and stay out of standing water near outlets or the panel.

Here's the order that actually matters:

1. Find the shut-off valve, a small valve on the cold-water line entering the top of the tank, and turn it clockwise until it stops.
2. Flip the breaker labeled for the water heater at your unit's panel, only if you can reach it without standing in water. If the panel is wet, leave the power alone and move to the next step.
3. If it's a gas unit, turn the gas control knob on the heater itself to off. That's separate from the breaker.
4. Pull anything off the floor nearby, rugs, boxes, chargers, power strips, away from the water line.
5. Crack a window or run the bathroom fan if the closet is enclosed. Humidity in a sealed condo climbs fast once water starts evaporating.

## Who do you call first, the AOAO, a plumber, or a water damage company?

Call your building's emergency maintenance or AOAO line first, since a tank failure in a high-rise affects shared risers and the unit below you. Call a plumber next for the tank itself. Call a water damage cleanup crew to extract water and dry the floors, baseboards and carpet before it reaches the unit below or starts growing mold.

Your AOAO (Association of Apartment Owners) manages the building's common elements, including the plumbing chases and the ceiling of the unit below yours, and most Honolulu high-rises post an emergency maintenance number in the lobby or the owner's packet. Call that number before anything else that touches shared plumbing. A plumber is who actually repairs or replaces the water heater; that part of the job isn't something a water damage crew handles. Once the leak itself is stopped, a dedicated cleanup crew extracts the standing water, dries the wall cavity and subfloor, and cleans or replaces the carpet before mold has a chance to set in. ## Why does a water heater closet flood the unit below in a Honolulu high-rise?

Water heater closets in Honolulu towers usually sit against a shared plumbing chase or directly above a neighbor's ceiling, so even a slow leak finds the path of least resistance through floor penetrations and conduit sleeves into the unit below.

High-rises in Waikiki, Kakaako and Ala Moana stack water heater closets vertically floor to floor so they can share a single plumbing riser. The concrete slab floors between units route water sideways along expansion joints and conduit penetrations long before it ever shows up as a stain on your own floor. That's why the unit below often finds out about your leak before you do, and why calling the AOAO fast matters, it gives the building a chance to check the ceiling below and start protecting that unit too. ## How do you dry out carpet, baseboards and walls after a tank failure?

Professional drying pulls standing water with extraction equipment, then places air movers and a dehumidifier to dry the carpet pad, baseboards and the wall cavity behind them, checked daily with a moisture meter until readings return to normal.

Carpet caught within the first 24 to 48 hours can usually be saved, extracted, and professionally cleaned rather than torn out; our [carpet cleaning](/services/carpet-cleaning/) crew handles that step once the structural drying is underway. Baseboards often get pulled, or small holes drilled behind them, to let air reach the wall cavity directly, since drywall alone can trap moisture for weeks if it's left closed up. Drying equipment typically stays in place and gets checked daily, following the drying principles in the [IICRC S500](https://iicrc.org/s500/) standard, until the wood, drywall and subfloor all read back to a normal moisture range, not just "feels dry."

## Whose insurance pays, your HO-6 policy or the AOAO master policy?

In most Hawaii condo associations, your individual HO-6 policy covers your unit's interior finishes, flooring and contents, while the AOAO's master policy covers the building structure and common elements, but the exact split depends on your association's declaration and your own policy wording.

That makes this genuinely worth checking rather than guessing. A sudden tank failure is usually treated differently than a slow leak that went unnoticed for weeks, and most policies draw a hard line between the two (see the Insurance Information Institute's overview of [what a standard homeowners policy covers](https://www.iii.org/article/what-is-covered-by-standard-homeowners-insurance)). This is general information, not legal advice. Pull your HO-6 declarations page and the association's master policy summary and call your own agent before assuming either side will pay. We cover the mainland-policy version of this question in more depth in [our guide to whether homeowners insurance covers water damage](/blog/does-homeowners-insurance-cover-water-damage/), and the same logic about sudden versus gradual loss applies to a condo unit.

## How fast does mold become a risk in Honolulu's humidity?

Mold can start growing on wet drywall and carpet pad within 24 to 48 hours, according to the [EPA's guidance on mold and moisture](https://www.epa.gov/mold/brief-guide-mold-moisture-and-your-home), and Honolulu's year-round humidity pushes that timeline even faster than it runs on the mainland.

A sealed condo closet with poor airflow is exactly the kind of space that grows mold first, especially behind baseboards or inside a wall cavity that looks dry on the surface. If the leak sat for more than a day before anyone found it, or if the closet has had musty odor since, it's worth a separate look with our [mold inspection and testing](/services/mold-inspection-testing/) service before you close everything back up.

Once the tank is shut off and the plumber is scheduled, the part that actually determines how this story ends is how fast and how thoroughly the water gets pulled out and the space gets dried. That's the piece worth getting right the first time.
