## How Much Does Burst Pipe Water Damage Cleanup Cost in Honolulu?

Most Honolulu burst pipe cleanups fall between $1,800 and $9,500, with condo unit-to-unit losses and multi-room spreads running higher. The table below shows typical ranges by scenario; your actual number depends on square footage, materials affected and how long the water had to work before extraction started.

| Scenario | Typical Range |
|---|---|
| Single room (bathroom/laundry), caught within hours, Category 1 | $1,200 to $2,800 |
| Bedroom or living area, water sat overnight, Category 1 | $2,800 to $5,500 |

## What Drives the Price of Burst Pipe Cleanup?

Three things set the final bill: square footage affected, how long the water sat, and the water's category. Everything else on the invoice, extraction time, drying equipment days, and monitoring visits, flows from those three variables.

A burst supply line almost always starts as Category 1, clean water, straight from the pipe. But if it sits for more than a day, or soaks into carpet padding, subfloor or baseboards before anyone notices, it can degrade toward Category 2, gray water, under the [ANSI/IICRC S500 standard](https://iicrc.org/s500/) that restoration crews use to classify a loss. Category 2 water usually means more material comes out instead of drying in place, and it adds an antimicrobial treatment step.

Equipment days are the other big line item. A typical single-room loss needs 3 to 5 days of air movers and an LGR dehumidifier running continuously, with a technician returning daily to log moisture readings in the walls and subfloor with a penetrating meter. Larger losses need more units running longer, which is why [flood equipment rental](/services/flood-equipment-rental/) line items scale with square footage, not with the number of rooms alone.

## Ceiling and Wall Cavity Drying vs Tear-Out: Which Costs More?

Drying a wet ceiling or wall cavity in place usually costs less than tearing it out, but only when the water was caught fast and stayed Category 1. Drying in place means small inspection holes, injection drying through the cavity, and air movement forced behind the drywall until moisture readings drop to a dry standard, no demolition, no rebuild.

Tear-out becomes the cheaper long-term option once insulation is saturated, drywall has started to sag or delaminate, or the water sat long enough to risk mold growth inside the cavity, which the [EPA notes can begin within 24 to 48 hours](https://www.epa.gov/mold/mold-cleanup-your-home) on wet building materials. At that point, drying in place just traps moisture behind an intact wall. Removing the wet material, drying the open cavity, and letting your contractor rebuild it is usually faster and more reliable.

For Waikiki and Kakaako condo towers specifically, a riser or supply-line failure on an upper floor often means the unit below needs ceiling cavity work regardless of category, since gravity carries the water down through the slab penetrations and electrical chases before anyone upstairs even notices the leak.

## Crawlspace and Post-and-Pier Drying in Older Oahu Homes

A soaked crawlspace under a post-and-pier home costs $2,500 to $6,000 to dry properly, more than a comparable slab loss, because the air underneath has to be actively moved and dehumidified, not just the visible flooring above it. Older plantation-era homes around Makiki, Kaimuki and Nuuanu were built this way, and a burst pipe under the house can run for days before anyone smells it.

Crews set air movers and a dehumidifier directly in the crawlspace, check the underside of the subfloor and any exposed joists with a moisture meter, and monitor daily until readings return to a dry baseline. Skipping this step is the single most common cause of mold calls that show up weeks after a homeowner thought the burst pipe problem was already solved; see [how quickly mold grows after water damage](/blog/mold-after-water-damage/) if you're unsure whether your timeline is a concern.

## Does Homeowners Insurance Cover a Burst Pipe in Hawaii?

Most Hawaii homeowners policies cover sudden, accidental water damage from a pipe that bursts without warning, which is exactly the kind of loss this guide covers. What they typically do not cover is a leak that was gradual, long-term seepage, or damage traced back to deferred maintenance, like a slow drip behind a wall that finally caused rot.

The distinction matters because adjusters look closely at how the water got there. Document the source, take photos before anything is moved, and start extraction and drying promptly: insurers expect homeowners to mitigate further damage once a loss is discovered. For the fuller picture on what a standard policy treats as sudden versus gradual, see [does homeowners insurance cover water damage](/blog/does-homeowners-insurance-cover-water-damage/) and the [Insurance Information Institute's overview of standard homeowners coverage](https://www.iii.org/article/what-is-covered-by-standard-homeowners-insurance). Every policy reads differently, so check yours before assuming either way.

## How to Read Your Water Damage Cleanup Estimate

A legitimate cleanup estimate breaks the job into line items you can actually verify, not a single lump number. Expect to see emergency water extraction (often billed per square foot or per hour), equipment rental days for air movers and dehumidifiers, daily moisture monitoring visits, antimicrobial application if the water tested Category 2, and disposal of any unsalvageable carpet, pad or drywall.

What you should NOT see on a water damage cleanup estimate is a charge for repairing, replacing, or re-piping the burst line itself. That part of the job belongs to a plumber and is quoted and billed separately. A restoration estimate covers the water's aftermath, extraction, drying, structural monitoring, and mold prevention, not the plumbing repair that caused it.
