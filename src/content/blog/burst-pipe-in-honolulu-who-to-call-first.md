Call a plumber to stop the source, and call a water damage restoration company like AAA Water Damage Restoration & Carpet Care to start extraction and drying. Both calls happen within the same hour. A burst supply line or failed water heater connection in an upper-floor unit here doesn't just soak your place, it runs down into the unit below, and that changes who you need to call and how fast.

## What should you do in the first 60 seconds?

Shut off the water at the source valve or the main, then cut power to any outlet or fixture sitting in standing water. Those two moves stop the damage from getting worse and prevent an electrical hazard while you figure out next steps.

If you're in a condo, the main shutoff for your unit's supply line is usually under a sink or near the water heater closet. If you can't find it or the leak is coming from a shared riser, shut off water at your unit's isolation valve and call your building's resident manager or AOAO immediately. This is a building-wide issue in towers across Waikiki, Kakaako, and Ala Moana, where one failed line on a high floor cascades through several units below.

Do not run a wet/dry vac on standing water near an outlet that's still energized. Do not step into water if you're unsure the breaker is off.

## Who do you call first, a plumber or a restoration company?

Call both, within the same hour, because they do two different jobs. A plumber stops the source: finds the failed line, fitting, or valve and repairs it so water stops flowing. A water damage restoration company removes the water that already got in and dries the structure before it causes secondary damage.

One without the other leaves a gap. A plumber who fixes the pipe but doesn't extract standing water leaves wet drywall, soaked carpet pad, and damp subfloor behind, exactly the conditions mold needs. A restoration crew can't start real drying work if water is still actively flowing from an unrepaired line.

Their crews handle the water removal, structural drying, and the insurance documentation adjusters ask for, not the pipe repair itself. Waiting on one call before making the other costs you hours that matter. Mold can begin to grow on wet drywall and carpet within 24 to 48 hours, according to the [EPA](https://www.epa.gov/mold/brief-guide-mold-moisture-and-your-home), so the clock on damage starts the moment the pipe fails, not when either contractor arrives.

## What happens in the first 24 hours after a burst pipe?

The first 24 hours are about stopping loss, not rebuilding. A restoration crew arrives, assesses which category of water you're dealing with (clean water from a supply line is Category 1, but it degrades fast once it sits or touches building materials), and starts extraction immediately with truck-mounted or portable equipment.

Next comes moisture mapping: a technician checks drywall, flooring, baseboards, and cabinetry with a moisture meter to find out how far the water traveled behind walls and under flooring, not just what's visible. Based on that map, they set up air movers and dehumidifiers sized to the affected square footage, following drying guidelines under the [IICRC S500 standard](https://iicrc.org/s500/) for water damage restoration.

Any materials that can't be dried in place (soaked carpet pad, swollen baseboard, saturated drywall near the floor line) typically get removed at this stage rather than dried, since drying them in place usually costs more than replacement and risks trapping moisture. Expect daily monitoring over the following several days until moisture readings return to normal for the materials involved. For more on the general timeline, see [what to do in the first 24 hours after water damage](/blog/what-to-do-first-24-hours-water-damage/).

## Does homeowners insurance cover burst pipe damage in Hawaii?

Most homeowners policies cover sudden, accidental water damage from a burst pipe, since it's an unexpected internal failure rather than gradual wear. Coverage typically does not extend to the plumbing repair itself, only the resulting water damage to the structure and contents.

Document everything before cleanup starts: photos of standing water, the source of the failure, and any damaged belongings. Keep your plumber's invoice and the restoration company's scope of work, since adjusters compare both against the claim. If the leak was gradual, from a slow seep rather than a sudden burst, insurers often deny the claim, so the distinction matters. For the full picture on what's typically covered and what isn't, see [does homeowners insurance cover water damage](/blog/does-homeowners-insurance-cover-water-damage/).

If you're in a condo or co-op, your unit's policy and the AOAO's master policy may split responsibility differently depending on where the failure originated. Ask your property manager for the master policy's water-damage language before you assume anything is or isn't covered.
