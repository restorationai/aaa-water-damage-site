**TL;DR:** If water is leaking through your ceiling from the condo above, cut power to that area first, then call your building's management or AOAO to get the source stopped. Call a water damage restoration company the same hour, not after the ceiling dries on its own, because wet drywall in Honolulu's humidity can start growing mold within 24 to 48 hours. Your HO-6 policy and the association's master policy split coverage differently, so document everything with photos and timestamps before anyone starts cleanup.

If you're standing in your living room in Kakaako or Waikiki right now watching a brown stain spread across the ceiling, or water is actively dripping onto your sofa, you don't have time to research condo insurance law. You need three things in order: get the water stopped, get the right people notified, and get drying started before the drywall and carpet pad become a mold problem. Here's that order.

## What do I do in the first five minutes water is coming through my ceiling?

Cut power to any outlet or fixture near the leak before you touch anything wet, since water and electricity in a ceiling cavity is a shock risk. Move furniture, electronics and rugs out from under the drip, and set a bucket or trash can to catch the active flow.

Take photos and a short video right away, with your phone's date stamp visible if possible. This is the single easiest thing you can do to protect yourself later with both the association and your own insurer, and it costs you nothing.

If the ceiling is sagging or bulging, stay out from under it. A saturated drywall ceiling can collapse without warning, and that's a different emergency than a stain.

## Who do I call first, the upstairs owner, building management, or a restoration crew?

Call your building's management company or the AOAO (Association of Apartment Owners) board first, because they're the ones who can get into the unit above you and shut off the source. In most Honolulu high-rises that's a 24-hour on-call number posted at the front desk or in your resident portal.

Knock on or call the upstairs owner directly too, if you know them, since a fast heads-up often gets the valve shut off minutes sooner than waiting on a property manager callback. Neither of those calls handles the water that's already in your unit, though. For that you need a restoration company that can get air movers and extraction equipment running the same day. ## Does my HO-6 policy or the AOAO master policy pay for this?

Generally, the AOAO master policy covers the building structure (the ceiling drywall, the concrete slab, common plumbing), while your own HO-6 condo policy covers your personal property, flooring, cabinetry and any upgrades you made inside the unit. Where the line falls depends on your association's governing documents and your specific policy, so this isn't legal advice, just the usual pattern.

Many HO-6 policies also include "loss assessment" coverage, which can help if the association passes along a shared repair cost to all owners. The [Insurance Information Institute](https://www.iii.org/article/what-is-covered-by-standard-homeowners-insurance) has a plain-English rundown of what a standard policy typically covers and excludes. Read your own declarations page and the AOAO's bylaws, or call your agent, before you assume either side is paying.

## How do crews dry out concrete ceilings and walls in high-rise condos?

Drying a concrete or drywall ceiling in a Honolulu high-rise usually means pulling the wet drywall where it's saturated, running air movers to create airflow across the open cavity, and placing a dehumidifier to pull moisture out of the room air. Concrete itself holds moisture longer than wood framing, so drying can take longer than a typical mainland wood-frame job.

Technicians use a moisture meter to track the drywall, baseboards and any exposed concrete day by day, following the drying principles in the [ANSI/IICRC S500 water damage standard](https://iicrc.org/s500/), the industry reference for structural drying. The goal is getting materials back to a normal moisture reading, not just visually dry, since a surface can look dry while the cavity behind it is still wet.

## What should I document for the building and my insurer?

Photograph the damage from multiple angles before anything is moved or cut out, and keep a running log of dates, who you called and when, and any receipts for fans, buckets or hotel stays if the unit becomes unlivable. Both the AOAO's insurer and your own HO-6 carrier will ask for this.

Our [guide to documenting flood damage for insurance](/blog/document-flood-damage-for-insurance/) walks through the specific photo angles and inventory details adjusters actually use, and the same checklist applies to a ceiling leak claim. A detailed moisture map and drying log from a restoration company also carries weight with adjusters, because it shows exactly what got wet and when it dried.

## How fast can mold grow in Honolulu's humidity after a ceiling leak?

Mold can begin growing on wet drywall and insulation within 24 to 48 hours, according to the [EPA](https://www.epa.gov/mold/mold-cleanup-your-home), and Honolulu's year-round humidity gives it even less of a head start than drier mainland climates. A leak that sits overnight in a Waikiki or Ala Moana high-rise unit is already on the clock.

If the ceiling cavity stayed wet for more than a day or two before anyone caught it, or if you start noticing a musty smell after the visible water is gone, a mold inspection is worth scheduling before you close up the drywall again.

If the ceiling is still dripping after power is off and the upstairs source is addressed, getting a water damage assessment started the same day keeps a contained leak from turning into a mold remediation job two weeks later.
