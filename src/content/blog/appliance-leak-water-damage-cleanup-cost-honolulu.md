## How much does appliance leak water damage cleanup cost in Honolulu?

Most appliance leak cleanups in Honolulu cost between $1,200 and $9,000, with condo-to-condo leaks running higher still. The exact figure depends on the water's contamination level, the square footage affected, and how many materials have to come out versus dry in place.

| Scenario | Typical cost range |
|---|---|
| Single room, clean (Category 1) supply-line leak, drywall and carpet only | $1,200 - $3,000 |
| Washing machine or dishwasher leak (Category 2), water into cabinets or subfloor | $2,500 - $5,000 |
| Water heater failure, water traveled across multiple rooms or under flooring | $3,500 - $7,500 |
| Appliance leak that reached the condo unit below (ceiling, drywall, flooring) | $6,000 - $15,000+, often split between the AOAO master policy and the unit owner |
| Extensive hardwood, laminate, or slab moisture needing specialty (Class 4) drying | $4,500 - $9,000 |

These are industry-typical ranges for Oahu, not a quote. Every loss is different, and a written scope after an on-site assessment is the only reliable number. ## What drives the price up or down?

Three things move an appliance leak estimate more than anything else: how contaminated the water was, how far it traveled, and how many days it takes to dry in Honolulu's climate.

**Water category matters because it determines what gets saved.** A supply-line failure, like the hose feeding your fridge's ice maker or the cold-water line to a washer, starts as Category 1, or clean water, under the [ANSI/IICRC S500 standard](https://iicrc.org/s500/). A dishwasher or washing machine leak that comes from the drain side, carrying detergent residue, food particles, or lint, is Category 2, or gray water. Category 2 water usually means more porous material, carpet pad, baseboards, cabinet toe-kicks, gets removed rather than dried in place, which adds labor and material cost.

**Distance traveled changes the scope fast.** A leak that stays under one appliance and a few feet of flooring is a contained job. One that wicks under laminate into a hallway, soaks into kitchen cabinet boxes, or drips through a ceiling into the unit below turns into a multi-room or multi-unit job with more demolition, more drying equipment, and more coordination.

**Drying days cost real money in Oahu's humidity.** Interior relative humidity in Honolulu runs high year-round, so dehumidifiers and air movers often run longer here than they would in a drier mainland market. Mold can begin to grow on wet drywall within 24 to 48 hours, per the [EPA's guidance on mold and moisture](https://www.epa.gov/mold/brief-guide-mold-moisture-and-your-home), which is why fast extraction matters more in a Waikiki or Kakaako high-rise than almost anywhere else. More drying days means more rental days on equipment, which shows up in the final invoice.

## Does insurance cover an appliance leak, and who pays in a condo?

Most homeowners policies cover a sudden, accidental appliance failure, like a supply line that bursts overnight, but not a slow leak that ran for weeks before anyone noticed. The distinction between sudden and gradual is the single biggest factor in whether a claim gets paid.

If your water heater tank splits or a washer hose blows out without warning, that's typically treated as a covered peril. If a slow drip behind the dishwasher has been staining the cabinet for months, most policies exclude it as a maintenance issue. Document the leak with photos as soon as you find it and keep receipts for any mitigation work, since you have a duty to limit further damage once you know about it. Our [plain-English guide to homeowners insurance and water damage](/blog/does-homeowners-insurance-cover-water-damage/) walks through what adjusters typically ask for, and the [Insurance Information Institute](https://www.iii.org/article/what-is-covered-by-standard-homeowners-insurance) has a general overview of what standard policies cover.

Condo and high-rise living in urban Honolulu adds a wrinkle. When a leak in your unit damages the unit below, or a leak from above damages yours, responsibility usually splits between the building's AOAO master policy and each owner's individual HO-6 policy, depending on where the water originated and what the association's governing documents say. That split is rarely obvious from the outside, which is why getting a clear moisture map and written scope early matters. Our post on [who to call first for a water heater leak in a Honolulu condo](/blog/water-heater-leak-honolulu-condo-who-to-call/) covers how that coordination typically plays out.

## What happens during professional cleanup?

Professional appliance leak cleanup follows a set sequence: stop the source, extract standing water, dry the structure to a verified standard, then repair.

1. **Shut off the water and assess.** The appliance's supply valve gets closed, and a technician checks how far the water traveled using a moisture meter, including behind cabinets and under flooring.
2. **Extract standing water.** Any pooled water gets pulled with extraction equipment before drying equipment goes in, since trapped water under flooring slows everything down.
3. **Remove unsalvageable material.** Soaked carpet pad, swollen particleboard, or Category 2-contaminated baseboard typically comes out rather than being dried in place.
4. **Set drying equipment and monitor.** Air movers and dehumidifiers run until moisture readings in the affected materials return to a dry standard consistent with the [ANSI/IICRC S500 guidelines](https://iicrc.org/s500/), with daily or every-other-day moisture checks.
5. **Document for insurance.** Photos, moisture logs, and a written scope get compiled for the claim, whether that claim goes to a personal policy or an AOAO master policy.
6. **Reconstruct.** Drywall, flooring, and cabinetry get replaced once the structure reads dry.

An appliance leak that's caught early and limited to clean water can be dried without removing much material at all. One that sits for days, or that carries gray water into porous surfaces, almost always costs more because more has to come out before drying can even start.

If you're standing in water right now, shutting off the appliance's supply valve is the first move, before anything else. From there, a scoped assessment is the only way to know whether you're looking at the low end of that table or the high end.
