## What Does a Burst Pipe Cleanup Company Actually Do (vs a Plumber)?

A plumber stops the water and repairs or replaces the failed pipe. A burst pipe cleanup company extracts the standing water, dries the structure, and documents the loss for insurance, and those are two trades, not one company wearing two hats.

In Honolulu's condo towers, this split matters even more. A failed supply line on the eighth floor of a Waikiki or Ala Moana building doesn't just soak one unit, it often runs down through ceilings and walls into the units below. The plumber's job ends once the leak is stopped. The cleanup crew's job is pulling moisture out of everything the water touched before it turns into a mold claim, and in Hawaii's humidity that window is short: the [EPA notes mold can begin growing on wet materials within 24 to 48 hours](https://www.epa.gov/mold/brief-guide-mold-moisture-and-your-home).

AAA Water Damage Restoration & Carpet Care handles the extraction, structural drying, moisture mapping, and mold-prevention side of a burst pipe loss anywhere on Oahu. The pipe repair itself goes to a plumber, and a cleanup crew that tells you otherwise is overselling its scope.

## What Should You Ask Before Hiring a Burst Pipe Cleanup Company?

Ask whether they'll give you a written drying plan with daily moisture readings, not just a verbal "it's drying fine." That document is what protects your insurance claim and tells you when the job is actually done, not just when the fans get picked up.

A few specific questions that separate a careful crew from a rushed one:

- **"Will I get moisture readings in writing, each visit?"** A competent crew logs numbers with a moisture meter (and often a thermal imaging camera to find water hiding behind walls or under flooring) on day one, then again at every visit, so you can see the structure actually drying rather than taking someone's word for it.
- **"How fast can someone be on-site?"** AAA answers 24/7, which matters in a tropical climate where a delayed response of even a day can be the difference between drying in place and ripping out drywall.
**"Who handles the pipe, and who handles the water?"** If one company claims to do both the plumbing repair and the structural drying, ask to see that split in writing. In Hawaii, these are typically two separate scopes of work.
- **"Do you work directly with my condo's AOAO or building engineer?"** In a high-rise loss, the building's insurer, the AOAO board, and your own homeowners or HO-6 policy are often all involved. A crew that has done condo work before will know to loop in the building engineer before opening walls or running equipment in common areas.

## What Are the Red Flags in a Burst Pipe Cleanup Quote?

The biggest red flag is a crew that wants you to sign something before they explain what it does. An assignment of benefits (AOB) form hands your insurance claim rights directly to the contractor, and once it's signed, you may lose the ability to negotiate your own settlement.

Other signals worth pausing on:

- **No written scope or drying log.** If a company can't produce daily moisture numbers, you have no way to confirm the structure is actually dry before they close out and leave.
- **Pressure to sign anything on the spot, especially an AOB.** Read it, or better, don't sign it at all until you've talked to your insurer. The [Insurance Information Institute has a plain guide to how disaster claims typically get settled](https://www.iii.org/article/settling-insurance-claims-after-a-disaster).
- **Vague answers about licensing split.** If a company implies they'll "handle everything including the pipe" without naming the plumber doing that work, get it in writing before anything starts.
- **No follow-up visits.** Drying a Honolulu interior usually takes several days of monitoring, not a single visit with fans left running unattended.

## How Does Condo and AOAO Coordination Work in a Burst Pipe Loss?

In a condo or high-rise, the cleanup crew needs to coordinate with the building's AOAO and resident engineer before equipment goes into hallways or common walls, because the building's master policy and the unit owner's HO-6 policy often split responsibility differently than a single-family home claim would.

That's common across Waikiki, Kakaako and Diamond Head towers, where a single failed riser or supply line can affect several units stacked vertically. A crew experienced with these buildings will document moisture readings unit by unit and keep the building engineer informed of equipment placement, which keeps the claim moving instead of stalling over who's responsible for what. Drying is typically carried out in line with the [ANSI/IICRC S500 standard for water damage restoration](https://iicrc.org/s500/), the industry reference most insurers and restoration crews work from.

## What Should You Do Next?

If water is actively running, shut off the main or the fixture's supply valve first, then call a plumber for the pipe and a cleanup crew for the water, at the same time if you can. Waiting to see how bad it is before calling either one is the single most common mistake homeowners make in a burst pipe loss.
