## What should you look for in a Honolulu mold remediation company?

Look for a company whose primary business is restoration and remediation, with local experience in how Oahu's building styles trap moisture. Single-wall construction in older Makiki and Moiliili walk-ups, and high-rise shared plumbing in Waikiki and Kakaako towers, behave very differently when water gets in, and the containment approach should match the structure.

Ask how they handle the moisture source, not just the visible mold. Scraping and painting over a mold patch without fixing the leak or the humidity driving it means the problem comes back within weeks in Honolulu's climate. A legitimate remediation process includes moisture mapping, containment with poly sheeting, HEPA air filtration, and a final verification step before the area is called clear.

Ask whether they coordinate directly with your insurer or your AOAO board if you're in a condo. Mold claims in multi-unit buildings often hinge on proving where the water came from and when, which is why documentation discipline matters as much as the physical cleanup.

## What does mold remediation actually involve?

Mold remediation is a multi-step process: find and fix the moisture source, contain the affected area, remove the material that can't be cleaned, and verify the air and surfaces are clear before closing the job.

A typical job starts with an inspection to locate the moisture source and the extent of growth, often using a moisture meter and, where needed, air sampling. The crew then seals off the work area with plastic sheeting and runs negative air pressure so spores don't spread to the rest of the home. Porous materials that have absorbed mold, like drywall or carpet padding, usually get removed rather than cleaned, while hard surfaces are treated and HEPA-vacuumed. The job closes with a final check, and in many cases a third-party clearance test, before the area is considered ready to rebuild.
