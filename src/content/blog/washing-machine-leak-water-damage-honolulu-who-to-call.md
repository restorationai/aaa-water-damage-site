If you're standing in a laundry room right now watching water creep toward the hallway, you don't need a long explanation, you need two numbers and a clear order of operations. A washing machine leak is almost always one of three things: a burst supply hose, an overflowing drain line, or a cracked drain pan, and each one points to a slightly different fix. But the water cleanup itself works the same way no matter which failure caused it, and getting the wrong company on the phone first wastes the hours that matter most.

## Which call comes first, the plumber or the restoration crew?

Call the restoration crew first if water is actively spreading across flooring, soaking a wall, or running toward a neighboring unit. A plumber fixes the hose, valve, or drain line that failed. A restoration company stops the water from destroying your floors, cabinets, and the unit below yours. In a Honolulu condo, both calls usually need to happen within the same hour.

Before either call, shut off the water. Most washers have two angle-stop valves behind the machine, one hot and one cold. Turn both clockwise until they stop, then unplug the washer at the outlet. If you can't find the valves or they won't turn (common in older Makiki and Moiliili walk-ups with corroded fittings), shut off the water at the unit's main shutoff instead and let the arriving crew handle the rest.

A burst supply hose sprays fast and the damage is usually contained to the laundry area and whatever floor it reaches. A drain overflow or a cracked drain pan tends to pool slowly and migrate under cabinets and flooring before anyone notices, which is why vinyl plank and laminate floors often look fine on top while the subfloor underneath is already saturated. Either way, [emergency water removal](/services/emergency-water-removal/) needs to start the same day, not after the floor has had 48 hours to wick moisture upward.

## What happens when you call?

When you call, expect the dispatcher to ask what's leaking, how long it's been running, and whether water has reached an outlet, a wall, or a neighboring unit. Have that information ready, it tells the crew what equipment to bring before they walk in the door.

On arrival, a technician checks the source is actually off, then uses a moisture meter to map how far the water traveled under flooring and behind baseboards, not just what's visible. Extraction starts immediately with truck-mounted or portable equipment, followed by air movers and dehumidifiers positioned to dry the structure according to the [ANSI/IICRC S500 standard](https://iicrc.org/s500/), the industry benchmark for water damage drying. For a straightforward single-room leak caught early, that drying equipment typically stays in place three to five days with daily moisture checks, longer if flooring has to come up.

## Is washer drain water safe to clean up yourself?

No, washer drain and overflow water is classified as Category 2 (gray water) and should not be mopped up with household towels alone. Gray water carries detergent residue, lint, and bacteria from the drain line, and it needs to be extracted and the affected materials sanitized, not just dried.

A burst supply hose is different. That's Category 1, clean water straight from the supply line, with a lower contamination risk if it's addressed quickly. But clean water doesn't stay clean. The longer it sits on carpet, under flooring, or inside a wall cavity, especially in Honolulu's year-round humidity, the faster it picks up contaminants and the more it behaves like a Category 2 loss. A pro will tell you which category applies once they see how long the water sat and what it touched.

## What about stacked units in Honolulu condos?

If you're in a high-rise in Waikiki, Kakaako, or Ala Moana, a washing machine leak on your floor is also a ceiling problem for the unit below. Vertical plumbing stacks and shared floor assemblies mean water doesn't stay contained to your square footage, it travels down through the slab and into the ceiling of the unit underneath within hours.

Tell your building's resident manager or AOAO as soon as you call for cleanup. Most association insurance policies draw a line between your unit's contents and the building's structure, and documenting the source and timeline early keeps that split from becoming a dispute later. If you're dealing with the reverse situation, water coming from above, [this guide on water leaking from the condo upstairs](/blog/water-leaking-from-condo-upstairs-honolulu-who-to-call/) walks through who's responsible for what.

## How do you document it for insurance?

Photograph the source of the leak, the hose or valve itself, before anyone touches it, then photograph the water's spread across flooring and up walls. Most homeowners and condo-contents policies cover sudden, accidental water damage like a burst supply hose, but they typically exclude damage from gradual leaks or deferred maintenance, so the timeline matters as much as the photos.

Keep the hose or failed part if a plumber removes it, note the date and time you discovered the leak, and save your restoration company's moisture logs and scope of work.
