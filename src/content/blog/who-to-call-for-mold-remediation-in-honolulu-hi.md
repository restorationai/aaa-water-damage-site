## Is this a wipe-down job or a remediation job?

A few square feet of surface mold on a bathroom tile or window sill is usually a cleaning job, not a remediation job. The [EPA's mold cleanup guidance](https://www.epa.gov/mold/mold-cleanup-your-home) says small areas, generally under about 10 square feet, can often be cleaned with household detergent and water by the homeowner, as long as the surface is non-porous (tile, glass, hard plastic).

It stops being a DIY job once mold is on a porous material (drywall, carpet, upholstered furniture), covers more than roughly 10 square feet, keeps coming back after cleaning, or smells musty from somewhere you can't see. At that point you're dealing with active moisture intrusion inside a wall cavity or under flooring, and wiping the visible patch just hides the problem while it keeps growing. Single-wall construction, common in older Kaimuki, Makiki, and Moiliili buildings, wicks moisture fast, so mold behind a wall in these homes often outpaces what's visible on the surface.

## Mold inspector or remediation company: which do I call first?

Call a remediation company directly when you already know the source, like mold spreading from a known leak; call for inspection and testing first when you can't see or locate the moisture problem. If a water heater failure, a slow pipe leak, or a roof intrusion is the obvious cause, you don't need a test to confirm there's mold, you need the water stopped and the affected material removed.

Call for [mold inspection and testing](/services/mold-inspection-testing/) first when you smell mold but can't find it, when a recent water event has you worried about what's happening behind the drywall, or when you're buying or selling a property and need documentation. An inspector uses moisture meters and sometimes air or surface sampling to map where mold actually is before anyone starts cutting into walls. If you recently had a leak and want to understand the mold timeline, [how quickly mold grows after water damage](/blog/mold-after-water-damage/) walks through the 24 to 48 hour window that makes Honolulu different from drier mainland markets.

## Renting or in a condo: do I call the landlord, the AOAO, or a remediation company?

As a tenant, notify your landlord in writing as soon as you spot mold or a leak, because most Hawaii rental agreements make the landlord responsible for habitability repairs, and a written notice starts that clock. As a condo owner, check your association's governing documents before assuming who pays.

In Honolulu's high-rise towers in Waikiki, Kakaako, and Ala Moana, a failed riser or a leak from the unit above is often a common-element issue the AOAO's insurance should address, while mold confined to your own unit's finishes may fall to your own HO-6 policy. These distinctions vary by building and by governing document, so this is general information, not legal advice specific to your lease or your association's CC&Rs. Either way, a remediation company can still assess and treat the mold while the ownership question gets sorted out separately; you don't have to wait on that paperwork to stop active mold growth. For the cost side of that conversation, see [does homeowners insurance cover mold remediation](/blog/does-homeowners-insurance-cover-mold-remediation/) if it's listed, or ask directly what your HO-6 or the AOAO master policy covers.

## What happens when you call AAA Water Damage for mold remediation?

The first call is mostly information gathering: where the mold is, roughly how large the area is, whether there's an active leak, and whether anyone in the home has respiratory sensitivities. Have your address, a rough description of the affected rooms, and photos ready if you have them; that lets the crew arrive prepared instead of improvising.

On site, the process generally follows the same order industry guidance recommends: find and stop the moisture source first (no remediation holds if water keeps coming), set up containment with poly sheeting to keep spores from spreading to clean areas, remove the affected porous material, dry the space with air movers and dehumidifiers, and document the work with photos for your insurer or property manager. This sequence mirrors the containment-removal-drying-documentation approach outlined in industry references like the 's mold remediation standards. ## How do I vet a mold remediation company in Hawaii?

Check the state's [DCCA Business Check](https://cca.hawaii.gov/business-check/) tool before hiring any contractor, which lets you confirm a business is registered and look up any complaint history before you let someone start cutting into your walls. It takes two minutes and it's free. Beyond that, ask what the written scope covers, whether they test after remediation to confirm the area is clear, and whether they'll document the work in a format your insurance or AOAO will accept.
